B2 · Central organizations: Requirements

The feature: an organization's owner creates it through sign-in and administers its real Central storage: create a workspace, find every workspace, and read the organization's complete history. Organizations stay separate. The owner can understand an interrupted setup or renewal, and an idle organization's stamp survives a signing-key change.

This page states the desired outcome and its authority. The Audit inventories the implementation and historical evidence; Specification, Program Design, Verification Design, Plan and Decisions complete the set. The Lead approved this cut; product implementation still requires the owner-selected branches and its own go-ahead.

The three things for you

  • Ready means Central confirmed setup. A lost answer does not become success, and the owner can explicitly retry without creating another organization.
  • Administration is complete: workspaces and history remain reachable beyond 100 records, including every record kind the current Central produces. A member or another organization's owner gets no authority over this organization.
  • Renewal and key changes are operationally finished: the owner sees what is pending or blocked, and an old key cannot be retired while any organization still needs it to start.
  • The actual page works on both real local host pairings. The existing isolated-DOM proof is retained at its own boundary.

Three answers remain open: first-answer retry versus today's one-day recovery; key maintenance versus a live fence; and engine compatibility here with a named later whole console versus the whole console here after complete additional design. Decisions shows gains, costs, recommendations and unselected pickers. Dependent obligations wait; remaining outcomes stay required.

Already decided: workspaces have descriptions and unique names. Old data is backed up, with later manual selection rather than automatic migration. Renewal status becomes visible under the administration surface's existing authorization; showing status adds no automatic repair.

What the owner needs to accomplish

These are three parts of one feature. All are required; finishing one does not accept the others. The owner is the direct user. Organization members and other organizations are protected stakeholders whose data and permissions must remain unchanged by a refusal.

Key: blue is the user, amber the capabilities, teal their source needs. The technical realization stays in the later Program Design; this map names what the owner gets.

This feature preserves the existing explicit Try again behavior; it introduces no workflow engine, daily job or automatic setup retry.

C3 · Keep the organization usable and explain interruptions

Renewing a used organization's stamp and changing signing keys are part of operating this feature. An unseen pending stamp can leave the owner unable to act; an old key retired too early can prevent an idle organization from starting. Existing stored facts should explain these conditions without exposing credentials or inventing another recovery service.

The complete current history contract

B2 reads the producer's closed contract. At Central e7b709f6, the valid kinds are organizationProvisioned, stampRenewed, workspaceCreated, machineEnrolled, machineRevoked, machineExpired and allowanceChanged. Origins are signInProvisioning, automatic and person; only person carries a person ID and checked permission. Source: organization_change_rows.rs:15–23,51–66,107–117.

Sign-in currently admits only the first three kinds and omits automatic (fleet-admin-client.ts:45–73). Completing this consumer is B2's scope. Machine events are read and displayed; their creation, expiry and revocation remain B3's behavior.

Recommendation for an unknown future kind or origin: preserve the existing closed-parser refusal, unknown: malformed-reply, and show history as unavailable. This is my implementation recommendation from the current contract, not a new owner decision. It keeps an unrecognized event from being silently omitted or assigned invented meaning. The new known variants must succeed; genuinely unknown variants must still be distinguishable failures.

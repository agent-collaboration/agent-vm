B2 · Central organizations: Audit

B2 finishes the owner's organization administration against real Central: create an organization, wait for its confirmed setup, create and find its workspaces, read all its records, and keep its signed organization stamp usable. Most of the service path exists. Complete lists, renewal visibility, safe key retirement and the actual administration browser journey are unfinished.

Back to the Fleet access overview. The seven-page set follows the Lead-approved cut: Requirements, Specification, Program Design, Verification Design, Plan and Decisions. Product implementation still needs its go-ahead and the selected owner branches.

The three things for you

  • One complete feature: the signed-in owner can administer the organization through the real page and private link on both host pairings. A small working API fixture is insufficient.
  • The lists are complete: more than 100 records cannot hide a workspace, and a valid kind of Central record cannot make the whole read unavailable.
  • Failures are visible and safe: a stamp awaiting an answer is distinct from a refusal or an expired pending stamp. An old signing key stays available until every organization's replacement survives a cold Central restart.

The retry promise is open. Approved B2 promises the first answer again without naming a retention limit. Today's implementation replays it for 24 hours, then checks whether the caller's workspace exists. These differ; neither this audit nor a merged PR supplies your answer. The exact conflict stays open for the Decisions page.

Your later backup-only, no automatic migration answer replaces the original same-store data promise. The new administration page will also expose the stamp status already retained by sign-in.

Two further choices: an owner-run key-maintenance window versus a live key-epoch fence; and engine compatibility here with a named later whole console versus the whole console here after additional complete design. All three comparisons and unselected pickers retain their costs. Production operations remain owner-run.

What “B2 done properly” means

The owner creates an organization through sign-in, sees it ready only after Central confirms, then creates a workspace and sees it in a complete list with its change record. A member's request and a request for another organization are refused. Lost replies remain uncertain until reconciled. Used organizations renew their stamps safely; blocked renewals are visible; changing the signing key cannot strand idle organizations.

Where the sources disagree: the retry promise

Key: teal is the approved wording, amber today's code, violet the still-open answer. Both branches retain the requirement to avoid a duplicate workspace.

Approved B2, flows promises that a repeated operation gets its original answer, and that a changed body/person/organization conflicts. It also says two operations for the same name return one workspace. The owner's later choice of descriptions and unique names replaces that latter behavior; today's duplicate is nameInUse.

Open: the Lead's targeted owner-message search recovered no first-hand decision selecting 24 hours; the nearest was the owner's question “how many ties we retry?” (Fleet transcript line 112302).

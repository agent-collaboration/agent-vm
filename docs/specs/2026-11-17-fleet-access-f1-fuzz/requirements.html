F1 · Fuzz testing for sign-in: Requirements

Why: sign-in is the front door. Hand-written tests check the cases someone thought of. F1 adds machine-generated inputs, action sequences and UI exploration, so the cases nobody thought of get tried too. They run against the real sign-in service on both hosts (workerd and Node), and every failure comes back as a small reproducible case.

U1Every endpoint a browser or CLI can reach on the sign-in service gets random, malformed and boundary request bodies. It answers only with its documented success or a documented refusal: never a crash (5xx), never someone else's data, never a session token in the body.Every person signing in; you, as the operator
U2Long random sequences of real storage operations, through the same Drizzle stores the service uses on both storage engines, never break the organization rules. Those rules: every organization keeps an owner; a person's chosen organization is one they belong to; a removed member keeps no session; the authorization fence only moves forward.Organizations and their members
U3Bombadil, Antithesis's UI fuzzer, explores the sign-in page (signed out) and the owner and People pages (signed in) in real Chrome, on both hosts. It checks rules a person would notice:
  • no crash or console error;
  • no secret on screen;
  • passkey stays click-only;
  • a member never sees the People list.

Boundary

  • In scope: the TypeScript sign-in service in the public Switchboard repo, on its local test hosts (workerd and Node), with software authenticators and controlled providers, as today's tests use.
  • Not in scope:
    • Rust crates and Turmoil: Central is Lead A's lane;
    • real Google or GitHub, physical authenticators, hosted Cloudflare;
    • performance or load testing;
    • changing CI gates. That's yours to approve (Decision F1-1).
  • Proof limits are the same as the existing local tests: what F1 proves holds on the local hosts, not on production.

How B1-B is built, in short

The two passkey sign-in requests become public. Better Auth's passkey plugin, with its existing user-verification hook, creates the session, and the service's session-create hook writes both factors into that same insert. The service strips the token from the answer and records the sign-in in its handler. A failure after the session exists answers 503 with no cookie, and the handler's clean-up deletes the session. The sign-in page gets Better Auth UI's PasskeyButton. A client request hook gives each challenge request a fresh Turnstile token. Journey 5 is proven in real Chrome with the virtual authenticator on a localhost origin, on both hosts, including a login started from the real owner CLI. The full detail is the Implementer's order above and the Program Design.

B1-B · Passkey sign-in: Audit

A full audit of what exists for passkey sign-in, and what needs fixing. Back to the Fleet access overview. B1-B is the second B1 deliverable there. It builds on B1-A's core and runs beside B1-C and B2.

The three things for you

A person can add a passkey today, but can't sign in with it. Everything that makes it safe is already built; the door is shut (the picture):

  • the service refuses the two sign-in requests as unknown routes;
  • the sign-in page offers only Google and GitHub;
  • a new passkey session isn't marked as confirmed, so it would still be asked for a second factor;
  • Better Auth's answer to a passkey sign-in carries the session token in its body, where page scripts can read it.

This feature changes the sign-in page: a third button, "Continue with Passkey". One choice is yours, on the Decisions page: whether that button also offers passkeys in the browser's autofill list, as Better Auth UI's button does, or only on click (recommended, with the reason).

The yardstick is your approved design. Your decision, in the approved Requirements line 270: OD21, decided by you on 31 October: yes. "A passkey with fingerprint, face or PIN signs you in and counts as both factors; Google or GitHub stays for the first sign-in and for anyone without a passkey."

Your journey 5 (U50, line 157): "Adding a passkey and signing in with it."

The findings come from two places:

  • the independent B1 review (B1 audit, finding R1-X2: "A registered passkey cannot be used to sign in");
  • my own reading of switchboard main at f3b2b88b and the pinned Better Auth 1.7.11 source on 15 Nov.

What went wrong, and what replaces it

  • Passkey sign-in was deferred and then called done with B1. B1 was accepted with the user-verification proof gate still open, so journey 5 couldn't run. The B1 audit names this as R1-X2.
  • A passing test hid the gap. The confirmation tests stayed green while sign-in was unavailable, and one test asserted the 404 as expected behaviour.
  • What replaces it: this feature. The 404 assertion becomes a refusal-without-UV assertion, and the "stays off" fallback is removed. Journey 5 is proven in a real browser on both hosts.

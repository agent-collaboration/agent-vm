B1-B · Passkey sign-in: Requirements

The feature: a person who has added a passkey signs in with it, by fingerprint, face or PIN. That one sign-in counts as both factors, so they can make a protected change straight away. Google and GitHub stay for the first sign-in and for anyone without a passkey.

The three things for you

  • A passkey sign-in without fingerprint, face or PIN is refused, and no session is created.
  • A passkey sign-in counts as both factors from that moment, so the first protected change isn't asked for a second factor.
  • The sign-in still passes Turnstile and the flood limit, like Google and GitHub.
  • Journey 5 runs in a real browser on both hosts: add a passkey, sign out, sign in with it, make a protected change.

The sign-in page gets a "Continue with Passkey" button under Google and GitHub. That's your OD21.

Nothing is open for you. On 15 Nov you chose click only: the button doesn't offer passkeys in the browser's autofill list (Decisions).

Not in this feature

  • Staging. Journey 5 on staging covers adding the passkey onward, under your R65, and that's BG's run. Passkey sign-in itself is checked locally, under R67.
  • Physical passkeys (a phone, a security key, Touch ID). Tests use Chrome's virtual authenticator and a software authenticator. A real device is your approved design's named limit.
  • Passkey-only accounts. A person still signs in with Google or GitHub first, as OD21 says.
  • The no-script fallback for adding and confirming passkeys (passkey-page-script.ts) is unchanged. Passkey sign-in needs the page's script.
  • B1-A's crypto and test-time rules apply here as built by B1-A. B1-B adds no crypto of its own: WebAuthn runs in the browser and in Better Auth.

B1-C · Organizations finished: Requirements

A person can see their organizations and choose the current one whenever they need to. An organization's owner can look up a person's membership there. The same person may belong to several organizations; choosing one grants no new permission and reveals nothing about someone else's organizations.

This feature completes organization choice and person lookup in the sign-in service. BG owns the complete setup and account pages that consume those capabilities. Fleet access overview.

The three things for you

  • Organization choice is still available after the first choice. It reads actual memberships and changes the native session's selection.
  • Only the requested organization's current owner can look someone up there. A member, another organization's owner, a removed person or an expired session learns nothing.
  • The lookup distinguishes a member and role, a current invitation, and no membership. It never reports that the person exists in some other organization.
  • Reading and choosing one's organization require no fresh second factor merely to read or choose. Inviting, canceling, accepting and removing keep their existing protection and records; this feature does not weaken those paths.

The lookup's placement, its subject input and the organization-choice component remain open owner decisions on Decisions; the account page remains free of admin actions under R62.

Better Auth continues to clear the active choice when a person submits a foreign organization. That existing behaviour is named and tested; preserving the previous choice would be a separate divergence, not a hidden implementation detail.

Who needs which read

Looking at one's own memberships and administering an organization are different capabilities. The first lets members choose where they are acting. The second lets an owner answer who belongs there; ordinary membership does not confer it.

What completing the feature means

The existing membership and session rows remain the source of truth. The feature is complete only when both the person's choice and the owner's lookup work through their real offered paths on both hosts.

Blue is the request; teal is authorization and the scoped read; green is an authorized answer; rose is refusal or inability to read. Unavailable never becomes “not a member.”

  • Choice: show organization name and the person's role, indicate the current selection, permit another real membership, and refresh the result after success or refusal. A normal read writes no selection. A fresh single-membership sign-in keeps selecting it as today; a fresh multi-membership sign-in requires a choice when the destination needs one.
  • Membership lookup: authorize before reading the subject; show only member with role, invited, or not-member. Only a pending invitation whose expiry is still ahead of the injected clock qualifies as invited. A canceled, rejected, accepted-with-no-current-membership or expired invitation does not establish an invitation or membership.
  • Input awaits the owner's answer: exact email is recommended because it covers an invited person before a user row exists. The alternative is an owner-only member/invitation picker with exact-email fallback for someone absent from the list. A list changes the author-proposed no-roster scope and needs its revised list/paging/privacy contract before implementation; neither option exposes another organization's people.
  • Failure: a missing/stale session or refused permission reveals nothing about the target; storage failure shows unavailable; a lookup does not mutate membership, invitations, roles or audit outcomes. A page must clear an earlier lookup result when the next request fails or changes organization.
  • Proof: use real native selection, live permission checks and injected Drizzle on local workerd/D1 and Node/better-sqlite3. Drive the delivered choice and lookup in real Chrome. Named wrong builds must fail: hide the chooser after selection; omit the organization filter; skip the live permission; return another-organization existence; call a storage error “not a member.” Existing mock/DOM tests remain useful at their actual layer.

The contract the getting-in pages receive

Green is B1-C's read, amber BG's flow decision, blue the person's choice and teal the native writer. BG never becomes another membership or selection store.

BG receives facts and a selection call, not a second definition of membership. B1-C proposes a request-local readPersonOrganizationContext result with three endings: signed-out, unavailable, or available. An available result carries choices: { organizationId, organizationName, role }[] and activeOrganizationId: OrganizationId | null, validated against those fresh memberships. The choice schema is derived from the existing membership schema; session tokens and other people's data are absent.

BG uses those facts in its own decideSetupPage and account/chooser pages. To change the current organization it calls existing POST /api/auth/organization/set-active with { organizationId }, keeps the native cookies and reads the context again. Better Auth owns the write and its refusal-clearing behaviour. BG keeps the checked return destination, invitation state and fixed CLI deadline. B1-C delivers and tests this capability on the existing served surface before BG replaces that surface; no temporary second membership store or flow engine is needed.

The owner-only readOrganizationPersonMembership is separate: requested organization plus proposed exact email in; member and role, invited, not-member, refused or unavailable out. It has no place on BG's non-admin account page.

The constraints that travel with the feature

All new sign-in rules stay in the runtime-free core, with host capabilities injected and no DI library. Reads use the injected Drizzle store; no raw SQL, new membership database or schema migration is proposed. Any new identifier is UUIDv7 from the injected ID source. Better Auth owns its native selection operation and remains unpatched; UI integration follows Better Auth UI as shipped and the existing Switchboard design system. These are the governing brief's standing constraints, not new owner decisions made by this writer.

Tests use controlled time and event waits; none reads or waits on the wall clock. The two real hosts remain distinct proof targets. Heavy runs go to Sunclaw. B1-A's allowed-globals and UUID checks cover new core code; its existing Hegel dependency supports B1-C's own four families, not a waiver of them. No new CI gate is added without the owner. The final design must name the old code it removes and keep the existing people-change and agent-path regression coverage.

The seven-page design now carries the complete recommended branch and the three explicit owner choices. The Lead approved the Stage 1 cut; the owner answers and independent design review still precede implementation. No product, release, staging or acceptance claim is made here.

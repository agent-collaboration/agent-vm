B1-C · Organizations finished: Specification

A person chooses among their own organizations; an owner looks up one person's membership in the organization they are administering. This page states the complete observable contract. The three open choices determine presentation and lookup input, not permission or privacy. No choice has been made for the owner.

The three things for you

The chooser remains usable after selection; the next read and native session agree. A lookup authorizes the caller before it reads its subject. An absent person and someone who exists only elsewhere get exactly the same answer. Failure is never reported as absence.

Three are open on Decisions: the lookup's page, how the owner identifies a person, and the organization-choice component. The detailed recommended design uses a separate owner page, exact email, and the shipped switcher with narrow server replies. Those recommendations are conditional, not approved defaults.

The authority is U9, U21/U46, U27 and U51, with approved R13/R14/R53/R58/R62. The owner's selected wording was “Your account page: your second factors, your organizations (choose one), and a clear ‘done’” (Fleet transcript:86867). Existing tests cover initial selection, not repeated choice or the missing lookup. All new proof here is planned.

The things the rules describe

These reuse the approved Fleet entity identities. A person's current choice is session context. Membership and permission still come from the sign-in service, and an invitation is not membership.

A session has at most one selected organization, while a person may have several memberships. The invitation's recipient is an email and need not yet have a person account.

Choosing an organization

Teal owns the read/check, blue is what the person uses, green is confirmed state, amber is the native refusal. A click or optimistic label alone does not establish the stored result.

S1 · A person reads only their own organizations

From U27/U51 and R62. A current, verified signed-in person MUST be able to read their membership choices, each with organization ID, name and their role, and the session's validated current selection. No token, credential, another person's membership or organization's roster is part of this self-view. No caller-supplied person ID selects whose memberships are read. The self-view has distinct signed-out, available and unavailable endings.

An available view with no memberships is an empty available view, not an error. A stored selection no longer present in the fresh memberships is represented as no current selection; this read does not repair the row. The configured first owner and an invited person may therefore still reach BG's organization step. Session expiry is enforced by the existing injected-clock session read.

S2 · Choice remains available after selection

From U51/R62 and R58. All current memberships MUST remain inspectable after a valid selection. A person in two organizations can choose the first and then the second in the same signed-in session, without starting sign-in again. The displayed current organization and the next organization-bound result MUST reflect the stored native selection after its read completes. Names and owner/member role labels are visible in the person's membership summary even if the selected switcher hides its own role badge.

Keep today's fresh-session rule: exactly one membership is selected at sign-in; several start unselected. A GET of the new self-context never silently selects one. A foreign-choice refusal may leave no current selection even with a sole membership, under S3; the person can select that membership again.

S3 · Selection uses Better Auth's own membership guard

Use the native set-active operation. Only a real membership can be selected. A signed-out or expired caller cannot select an organization. Selection creates no membership, changes no role, and stamps no second factor. Keep the pinned native foreign-choice effect: it refuses with 403 and clears activeOrganizationId; memberships and roles are unchanged. Re-read after both success and refusal, and do not restore the previous selection as though it were still authoritative.

Concurrent selections follow the native write order; no new lock or selection version is promised. The UI must not present an optimistic update as an authorization result. A later page entry/focus or explicit refresh reads the session and memberships again. No browser-persistent membership or selection store is added.

S4 · Reading and choosing do not demand a recent second factor

From U43/R53 and the owner's returning-sign-in answer. A valid person may read and select an existing membership with an absent or expired confirmation. These actions MUST NOT mint or extend confirmation. Keep initial authenticator setup ordering and protected-change checks: this rule does not make an unenrolled person ready or let them invite, cancel, accept, remove or create without the existing checks.

The owner lookup

The second authorization check catches a removal or session end while the subject read was held. It does not promise a transaction spanning separate reads; an answer describes the subject observation during that request.

S5 · Only the requested organization's current permitted person can look up a subject

From U21/U46, R13 and OD25. Require a current verified person session selected into the requested organization and a fresh native hasPermission check for organization:view, using the current member role with memory caching off. A role-name shortcut or a valid token signature is insufficient. In the current mapping only the owner holds that permission.

A member, an owner current only in another organization, a removed actor, an ended/expired session, or agent/bot/fleet-session credentials MUST be refused without any subject result. Run no subject read before authorization. Recheck session, selection and permission before returning successful subject data; a failed recheck discards that data. Lookups require no recent second factor, and call no central endpoint.

S6 · The answer is about only the organization requested

From U9/R14 and C4 (approved Specification:288). The answer is exactly one of:

  • member, with the person's owner/member role there;
  • invited, when there is a pending invitation there with expiry strictly after the injected clock's now;
  • not-member, otherwise.

Membership takes precedence over a pending invitation for the same email. If no member is observed, read and decode all invitation rows for that one organization/email; an older cancelled row cannot hide a newer current pending one. A person who belongs only elsewhere and a person with no account MUST have byte-identical successful negative bodies for the same requested organization. Reveal no other organization's ID, name, role, existence, invitation, user ID or counts. This is response equality, not a constant-time promise. Malformed stored role, invitation status/expiry or active-selection ID and storage errors are unavailable, never not-member or empty success. Only rows actually read are subject to that decoding obligation.

S7 · Lookup does not change people or settle history

From OD26 and existing people rules. The lookup MUST NOT create users, memberships, invitations, role changes, confirmation stamps or outcome records. It does not confirm which earlier operation completed. Existing invite/accept/cancel/remove, owner protection, no-resend and intent/outcome fault tests remain intact. No schema or migration is part of B1-C.

S8 · The input follows the owner's pending answer

The recommended exact-email option accepts one normalized exact email with the organization ID, in a POST JSON body, not a URL. It reaches invitations before a person row exists. Blank/invalid input gets a field error and no subject read; it is not a prefix or all-person search.

The alternative is an owner-only member/invitation picker with an exact-email fallback; the fallback is necessary to ask about someone absent from that list. It exposes a roster and therefore changes the author's proposed no-roster boundary. Its paging, public response and proof must be revised and reviewed before implementation if chosen; the current packet does not quietly enable native member-list routes. This alternative is a real scope/visibility choice, not a hidden setting.

S9 · The visible lookup follows the owner's pending placement answer

The recommended separate page has a “Look up a person” heading, the organization name, a labeled field or selected input, a Look up action and a live result/error region. It is linked from organization administration and guarded independently of that link. The alternative puts that same task inside the existing organization-admin page and requires a shared-shell adaptation that preserves B2's controls. Neither adds an admin action to the account page.

Clear an earlier result on input/organization change, submission, refusal, malformed reply or unavailable/network failure. A late reply for an older request MUST NOT overwrite the current request's result. A refused caller sees no subject information; a signed-out page request goes to sign-in without a subject email in its URL. No email, token or result body is recorded in telemetry or screenshots; browser proof uses test-only identities and redacted captures.

UI and integration constraints

S10 · The chosen organization component does not expose a directory

If the owner chooses the recommended shipped component, use Better Auth UI's unchanged OrganizationSwitcher with hideCreate, hidePersonal and hideSettings. Its nested organization views hide roles; a separate self-membership summary supplies the already-approved role labels. Open only the exact native GET list/full-organization reads its queries require, and release only whitelisted organization fields, to owners and members alike. Do not release members, invitations, totals, metadata or Fleet stamp fields. Keep the native membership guard and mutation unchanged.

The projection MUST be complete before any successful HTTP body reaches a script. A malformed native success is unavailable, not an unprojected fallback. A native full-organization read can itself clear a stale or foreign selection before refusing; preserve and disclose that native effect as well as S3's selection effect. The pure self-context read remains nonmutating. Internal server API reads retain their native full shape; only the public HTTP boundary projects it.

Those public native self reads require the same verified current stored-session authentication as the self-context. They accept no explicit organization, slug, member-limit or other query key; any query gets the same generic invalid-request answer before native dispatch. The shipped component is configured without a route slug and reads only the session's current organization. A caller cannot use native error differences to ask whether a foreign organization exists. Internal native query contracts are unchanged.

If the owner chooses the existing Switchboard chooser instead, use that approved exception and the self-context read; the native list/full routes stay closed. Do not build two permanent selectors or a runtime flag. Both options must satisfy S1–S4, and no choice permits opening native list-members.

S11 · BG consumes facts; it still owns getting in

B1-C supplies a current-person organization context, native selection and a reusable choice surface. BG owns decideSetupPage, first pending step, invitation/Decline, account-page shell, checked return destination, CLI deadline and all six combined local journeys. Preserve existing valid CLI continuation in the B1-C regression. When BG cuts over /owner-ready, it reuses these contracts and removes that old page; B1-C adds no competing setup engine, destination store or account route.

S12 · Both hosts, design system and full cutover

The real offered paths MUST work on local workerd/D1 and Node/better-sqlite3. The new surfaces use Switchboard's shared React/shadcn system, one top heading, labeled keyboard-reachable controls and 400 px dark-mode layout. Keep B1-A's injected crypto, clock, UUIDv7 and allowed-globals contract; tests use controlled time and event waits. Heavy runs go to Sunclaw. Add no CI gate. Remove the old hide-after-selection condition, superseded selector/controller for the chosen option, and obsolete route-closure assertions only where the chosen precise routes replace them.

S13 · Closed local machines, pure transitions and Hegel

From the 15 November owner ruling relayed in the updated brief and correction commission. Inventory the active-selection observation, invitation read view, per-request lookup answer and browser request lifecycle. Each MUST have schema-inferred closed state/event unions, an exhaustive pure transition(state, event, context) returning next state or a closed refusal reason, and explicit per-step invariants. Parse stored states at the boundary; unknown values fail loudly. Time comes through context. No state-machine library or effects runner is needed for these local machines.

Keep native Better Auth transitions and writes native. Selection clearing is an operation-local explanation of a fresh null observation, never a new persisted cause; ordinary null hydration is none. Concurrent native selection follows native commit order. Invitation expired is derived from a raw pending row and the injected clock; accepted/cancelled/rejected are distinct observations. Do not impose a pending-only cancel condition or terminal-state guarantee the pinned native writer does not have. The public lookup's strict expiry policy does not change native acceptance's equality boundary.

A candidate lookup answer MUST remain private until the final live check permits release. The browser MUST commit only a completion for its current pending generation; unmount terminates that lifecycle. Every project-owned local state assignment occurs only after the pure transition permits it. No new project-owned persisted state is added. Preserve native conditional invitation claims/rollback and native unconditional selection/cancel writes; do not wrap or patch them to fit a generic persistence rule.

Verification MUST include four complete Hegel event-sequence families with independent reference models, whole-state/result equality and invariants after every prefix; controlled clock moves, paired events and retries; deterministic settings with database disabled; semantic mutants and shrunk counterexamples; and compile-time exhaustiveness mutants. Force actual native race orders and decoder failures on both database hosts. Pure/model tests cannot replace that host or real-browser proof. Program Design and V8 specify the inventories, transitions, test files and exact limits.

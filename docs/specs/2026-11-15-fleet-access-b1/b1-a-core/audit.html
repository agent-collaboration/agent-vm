B1-A · One sign-in core, injected and honestly tested: Audit

A full audit of what was built for this feature, and what needs fixing. Back to the Fleet access overview. B1-A is the first B1 deliverable there, and B1-B (passkey sign-in) and B1-C (organizations finished) follow it.

The three things for you

Six parts of your design are only partly true today (the picture):

  • the core still takes crypto from the runtime;
  • tests use real time in two places;
  • one property-test family is missing;
  • "limited" is proven on a stand-in;
  • stored IDs are only spot-checked;
  • the agent path hasn't been re-observed.

The rows below say what "fully fixed" means for each.

None. Everything here is how the existing behaviour is built and proven.

The yardstick is your approved design (rules R30, R32, R33, R38 and R52, and needs U16, U23, U24, U25 and U42). Your own words in the approved Requirements:

  • "you inject stukff not pkul out engines" (U23, line 174);
  • "i dont allow wall clock time ever in tests" (U25, line 176);
  • "we ony use uuidv7" (U24, line 175).

The findings come from two places:

  • the independent B1 review (B1 audit, findings R1-X5, R1-X6 and R1-X7);
  • my own reading of the code at switchboard main f3b2b88b on 15 Nov, which a Terra review then checked.

What went wrong

  • Nobody added crypto to the host contract, so each new feature reached for the runtime's crypto. My acceptances of #10 and #12 didn't check R30.
  • The architecture check looks for runtime imports, not shared globals or .tsx files, so it couldn't catch any of this.
  • The browser test kept real-time bounds, and I accepted them in #13, against your rule.
  • The flood test used a stand-in, and I accepted it in #9, though your design names Miniflare's binding.
  • "Done" was claimed on narrow tests, not on your design's rows, so the missing Hegel family and ID readback weren't noticed.

What replaces it

This feature. Each row above becomes "fully fixed" as the Program Design describes and the Verification Design proves. The old code it replaces is removed: the six runtime-crypto calls, the five real-time bounds, the real-clock fixture rows, and the flood stand-in and its switch.

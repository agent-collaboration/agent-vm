Recorded risk: Miniflare's counter keeps its own real-clock window

Your journey SO2 accepted that "Miniflare's binding keeps its own clock". I read its source (miniflare 5.20260815.0-alpha, ratelimit-object.worker.js): it counts in windows of Math.floor(Date.now() / (period * 1000)), 10 or 60 seconds, aligned to the real clock. The test never reads that clock or waits on it.

The risk: if the requests in a "limited" case happen to cross a window boundary, the count resets and the case can't reach its 429 when expected. It would then fail: a false alarm, never a false pass.

How likely: roughly the case's duration divided by 60 seconds.

  • The social-sign-in case sends about 13 requests (outer limit 10 per 60 s), taking milliseconds, so it's very rare.
  • The page case sends up to about 301 requests (outer limit 300 per 60 s), one after another. If it takes a second or two, that's about one run in 30 to 60.

The Verifier records how long each case takes, so the real figure is known after the first run.

If it ever happens: the failure says the request wasn't limited. A rerun decides it. Nothing is waited on, and nothing passes wrongly.

Decided by you on 15 Nov: R30 covers our code, not Better Auth's own crypto

Open for you: nothing. This was the one open question, and you answered it: "but auth libary is trhe same on al platforms sso wehres the issue here?" and "yes do a." (Fleet session, 15 Nov). It's kept here so the choice and what it gave up stay visible.

What the question was

Before: R30 says "every sign-in rule … MUST live in one runtime-free core" that reaches crypto "only through capabilities its host supplies". B1-A routes all of our code's crypto through the host. Better Auth, the library our code is built on (R29), also does its own crypto inside: session tokens, sign-in state, two-factor codes. No decision of yours says whether R30 reaches inside the library.

Key: green the host's capability, amber our code, blue Better Auth, violet the library's own crypto.

What changes

Nothing beyond B1-A: our code's crypto goes through the host. Better Auth keeps doing its own.

What it improves

Better Auth stays as shipped, matching your rule of following Better Auth's own way. No patches.

What it costs

Some crypto inside the service doesn't go through the host, so a recording run can't see it.

Who bears it

The proof: U23's "every crypto call is recorded" covers our code, not the library's.

Picture

What changes

Better Auth's crypto is also routed through the host, through whatever hooks it offers, or by patching it where it offers none.

What it improves

Every crypto call in the service goes through the host and can be recorded.

What it costs

A larger feature, likely a patch or a fork of library internals, re-applied on every Better Auth upgrade.

Who bears it

Every later Better Auth upgrade.

Picture

"yes do a." Better Auth is the same library on both hosts and stays as shipped.

What it gave up: the crypto Better Auth does inside itself isn't recorded by tests. Ours is.

Would have cost: patching Better Auth and carrying the patch through upgrades.

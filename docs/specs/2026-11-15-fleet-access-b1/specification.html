B1: Specification

B1 must admit people only to their own organization, enforce the owner/member permission mappings, record each people change, and refuse unconfirmed changes. A user-verified passkey must eventually sign a person in; the current safety hold leaves that outcome missing. Shared central clauses are retained with their B1 boundary explicit.

Reading the status column: each label is the B1 audit's assessment of the named portion, not a fresh observation. defect carries the audit's contradicted verdict. Shared rows retain their complete original wording; a B1 status gives no delivery credit to their later central, machine-login or getting-in portions. Audit row IDs open the audit's outcomes ledger.

The approved source remains the shared Requirements, Specification, Program Design and Verification Design. This is their B1 split. Original IDs and full shared rows are retained so the boundaries can be inspected. The later decision register is inventory §1. Plans and acceptance claims supply no design authority here.

The entity picture shows identity and cardinality. The tables preserve the complete invariants and states. An organization’s sole creator-owner cannot be removed; a member has no permissions. Other referenced entity identities remain in the shared entity definitions.

R13Only a person whose role holds the permission (E27, U46) MAY: invite people to an organization or remove them (member); create workspaces; allow or withdraw a machine's allowance; approve or deny a device-code login; revoke a machine's enrollment; remove a session; set the token lifetime; and read the lookups (R14) and the who-is-logged-in view (R49).
In this design only the owner holds any permission, so "the admin" below means the owner. Only the deployment's configured owner MAY create an organization (A5, 3 November), and the creator becomes the new organization's owner. In this design an organization has one owner, its creator, who can't be removed or demoted; handing over comes later and MUST keep at least one owner. There is no fleet-wide role: the roles are owner and member, with managers to come (OD24, U46). Open sign-up, where anyone signed in may create an organization, waits with the people sign-in design's SD2.
The permission is checked live by the sign-in service, for the organization the action belongs to, with the person signed in to that organization and current there, at the time of the action as the people sign-in design's R9, R19 and R20 require (its freshness bound SD8); the confirmation it attaches names the permission it checked (OD25). Each change also needs R53's second factor. Creating a workspace is one step at Fleet central (4 November, B2), so it's checked and applied together, with no later application.
Each admin change MUST be recorded, as the people sign-in design's R12 requires. A change Fleet central applies is recorded in the same step as the change; a change whose record can't be written MUST NOT happen. A people change the sign-in service applies (inviting, removing) MUST be recorded before it happens, and refused if the record can't be written; its outcome is recorded after, and a record whose outcome was never written stays unconfirmed (OD26).
Anyone else MUST be refused with nothing changed and nothing revealed: a member, another organization's owner, a person no longer a member, a session no longer current, a bot or a session.

R53Step-up. Each change R13 reserves for the admin MUST be refused with nothing changed unless the admin's sign-in includes a second factor (E25) confirmed no more than 15 minutes (one setting) before the change. Those changes are: inviting or removing a person, changing a person's role, creating a workspace, changing a machine's allowances, approving or denying a device-code login, revoking an enrollment, removing a session and setting the token lifetime. The refusal MUST say that a second factor is needed.
One exception: approving a device-code login into an organization that has never had an enrollment MAY rely on the provider sign-in alone.
A second factor confirms on top of the same person's provider sign-in. A passkey sign-in with user verification counts as both factors, so its new session is confirmed from that moment (OD21); a passkey assertion without user verification MUST NOT sign in or confirm anything.
Reading (the lookups and the who-is-logged-in view) needs no second factor. A passkey confirms only with user verification.

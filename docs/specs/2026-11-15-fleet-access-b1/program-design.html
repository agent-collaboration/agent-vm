B1: Program Design

One runtime-free sign-in core owns people, permissions, second factors, records and request limits. Cloudflare and Node supply the database and runtime capabilities. Better Auth owns its native people tables; B1’s central client produces confirmations and provisioning calls, with a central stand-in for B1 proof.

Reading the status column: each label is the B1 audit's assessment of the named portion, not a fresh observation. defect carries the audit's contradicted verdict. Shared rows retain their complete original wording; a B1 status gives no delivery credit to their later central, machine-login or getting-in portions. Audit row IDs open the audit's outcomes ledger.

The approved source remains the shared Requirements, Specification, Program Design and Verification Design. This is their B1 split. Original IDs and full shared rows are retained so the boundaries can be inspected. The later decision register is inventory §1. Plans and acceptance claims supply no design authority here.

Passkey proof still required: B1 keeps public passkey sign-in off until the user-verification hook is proven at the pinned version. Registration and confirmation alone cannot close that missing sign-in outcome (R1-O2).

The host/core arrows mean supplied capabilities. They do not give hosts permission to add sign-in rules. The central stand-in observes B1’s producer only; B2 must prove the real interaction.

A people change has one actor and two records

The signed-in person is the actor. The permission and fresh-factor checks run before a change, then the before-hook writes intent. An unwritable intent prevents the change. A missing after-hook outcome leaves the intent unconfirmed; membership today never proves that old operation completed.

Keep a working factor through every change

The first verified app issues recovery codes. Later changes need a recently confirmed session; removal is decided conditionally in the store so two concurrent removals cannot leave zero usable factors. An unverified app is never the spare factor.

Count before any route changes state

The core’s outer gate covers its pages as well as plugin routes. Hosts supply a trusted normalized address; Cloudflare adds its shared location binding and Node counts in its process. Turnstile is checked when a social sign-in or passkey sign-in challenge starts, before its row is created.

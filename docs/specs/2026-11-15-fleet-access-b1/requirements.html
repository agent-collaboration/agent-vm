B1: Requirements

B1 gives people protected sign-in and organization membership. The configured owner creates the organization and manages its people; members belong with no powers. An authenticator app, recovery codes and later passkeys protect changes. B1 keeps today’s agent path working and runs the same rules on both sign-in hosts.

Reading the status column: each label is the B1 audit's assessment of the named portion, not a fresh observation. defect carries the audit's contradicted verdict. Shared rows retain their complete original wording; a B1 status gives no delivery credit to their later central, machine-login or getting-in portions. Audit row IDs open the audit's outcomes ledger.

The approved source remains the shared Requirements, Specification, Program Design and Verification Design. This is their B1 split. Original IDs and full shared rows are retained so the boundaries can be inspected. The later decision register is inventory §1. Plans and acceptance claims supply no design authority here.

People first; central comes later

B1 is in the sign-in service only. It creates no central store and forwards no central admin action. Its provisioning client talks to a stand-in for B1 proof; a real deployment remains settingUp until B2. Getting-in pages, all six browser journeys and staging automation have their own BG scope; they remain required in the shared design.
